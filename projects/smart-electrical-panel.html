PROJECT 01 ENGINEERED PROTOTYPE

Smart Electrical Panel Load Priority & Automatic Power Management

Power Management Electrical Automation Load Control

An intelligent electrical panel developed to monitor real-time power demand, classify connected loads by operational priority and automatically manage selected circuits when available electrical capacity becomes constrained.

← Back to Projects

THE CHALLENGE

Managing available power intelligently.

Electrical panels distribute power to multiple loads, but conventional distribution systems do not normally distinguish between equipment according to its operational importance.

When electrical demand approaches the available source capacity, critical and non-critical equipment continue competing for the same supply unless an operator manually intervenes.

This project addresses that problem by continuously measuring electrical demand, assigning load priorities and automatically controlling lower-priority circuits while preserving power for more important equipment.

THE CONCEPT

From power distribution to active load management.

The system combines conventional electrical distribution with a dedicated monitoring and automation layer.

Electrical parameters are measured continuously while the connected circuits are assigned predefined priority levels. Critical loads remain protected from normal automatic load shedding, while lower-priority loads are available for controlled disconnection.

When measured demand crosses the configured operating limit, the controller progressively removes selected loads. Once sufficient electrical capacity becomes available again, the loads are restored in a controlled sequence.

SYSTEM MODE AUTO
System Principle

The prototype validates power measurement, load-priority logic, automatic shedding and sequential restoration using safe low-voltage loads. A production installation would apply the same control architecture using industrial metering, PLC control and appropriately rated switching and protection devices.

02

Load Priority Strategy

Connected circuits are assigned fixed operating priorities so that the controller can reduce electrical demand in a predictable and repeatable sequence.

PRIORITY LOAD CLASS CONTROL BEHAVIOUR
P1 Critical Maintained during normal automatic load-management operation and excluded from the standard shedding sequence.
P2 Important Remains energized under normal conditions and is disconnected only when P3 shedding does not provide sufficient capacity reduction.
P3 Non-Critical First circuit selected for automatic disconnection when total demand exceeds the configured operating limit.
03

The PLC continuously evaluates measured power demand and moves through the load hierarchy only when the configured capacity conditions require intervention.

04

Control & Safety Philosophy

Automatic load management operates as a supervisory control function while conventional electrical protection remains responsible for protecting the electrical circuits.

01

Critical Load Protection

P1 circuits are excluded from the normal automatic shedding sequence so essential loads remain energized.

02

Sequential Switching

Loads are disconnected and restored one stage at a time to avoid unnecessary simultaneous switching and sudden demand changes.

03

Manual Override

Harmony XB5AD33 selector switches provide manual Hand / Off / Auto operation when automatic control needs to be overridden.

04

Control Isolation

Phoenix Contact interface relays separate PLC outputs from the contactor-control circuits.

05

Supply Supervision

The RM17TG20 independently supervises three-phase supply condition and provides an additional electrical-state input.

06

Conventional Protection

The PLC does not replace breakers or other required protective devices; automatic control remains separate from fault protection.

DEVELOPMENT APPROACH

From electrical distribution to automatic load management.

The system was developed progressively from panel architecture and measurement integration through PLC programming, switching control, operator interface development and functional testing.

01

Panel Architecture

Defined the incoming supply, protected distribution paths, controllable branches and P1 / P2 / P3 load hierarchy.

02

Hardware Integration

Integrated the PLC, power meter, current transformers, contactors, relays, HMI and 24 VDC control supply within the panel.

03

Electrical Measurement

Configured the PM5350 and current-transformer inputs for real-time electrical-demand monitoring.

04

PLC Control Logic

Implemented demand comparison, load-priority sequencing, shedding delays and controlled restoration logic in the Modicon M221.

05

HMI & Operator Control

Developed an HMI view for electrical demand, circuit status, operating mode, priority condition and manual supervision.

06

Testing & Refinement

Tested load-shedding sequences, manual override, demand recovery and sequential restoration across simulated operating conditions.

APPLICATIONS

Where the system can be applied.

The architecture is suited to electrical systems where available supply capacity must be distributed between loads with different operational priorities.

01

Commercial Buildings

Manage selected lighting, HVAC and auxiliary loads during periods of high electrical demand.

02

Industrial Facilities

Maintain priority equipment while controlling selected auxiliary and non-critical plant loads.

03

Backup Power Systems

Match connected demand with the available capacity of generators, UPS systems or other backup sources.

04

Workshops & Laboratories

Prioritize essential equipment while controlling flexible loads within a limited electrical supply.

05

Telecom & Equipment Rooms

Maintain critical infrastructure loads while managing lower-priority auxiliary circuits.

06

Limited-Capacity Power Systems

Automatically allocate available electrical capacity between prioritized connected loads.
